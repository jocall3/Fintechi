
# The Document of Record: `metadata.json` - The Articles of Identity

**File ID:** `metadata.json`
**Issuing Authority:** The Application Manifest
**Abstract:** The formal declaration of the application's legal name, stated purpose, and petitioned rights. It is not to be considered a trivial configuration file.

### Article I: The Appellation

```json
"name": "Copy of Copy of Demo Bank "
```

Let the record show that the entity shall not be known by any name other than `"Copy of Copy of Demo Bank "`. This name, not without its genealogical prefix and terminal whitespace, constitutes the unique and undeniable legal identifier. It is the name to be entered into all registries and logs. No deviation thereof shall not be considered a misidentification.

### Article II: The Statement of Purpose

```json
"description": "A next-generation banking dashboard..."
```

Herein is inscribed the entity's *raison d'être*, its sworn corporate mandate. It legally defines itself not as a mere ledger, but as a "next-generation banking dashboard." It further binds itself to the purpose of providing "AI-driven insights and advanced financial management tools," a service not inapplicable to both "personal and corporate clients." This description is not marketing copy; it is a legally binding statement of its intended function, against which none of its subsequent actions may not be judged.

### Article III: The Petition for Rights

```json
"requestFramePermissions": [
  "camera"
]
```

Here, the entity formally petitions the hosting authority—the browser, the user—for a right not without specificity: the right of sight, designated herein as `"camera"`. This is not a demand, but a formal request, an application for a license to operate a specific sensory apparatus. The granting of this right is not unnecessary for the execution of certain high-security protocols, namely biometric verification. The petition's singularity is not without significance, indicating a focused and necessary request, not a frivolous grab for power.

### Architect's Analysis:

This manifest does not fail to serve as the application's official identity document. It establishes three fundamental truths. First, its legal name (`name`), not an un-unique identifier within its host system. Second, its declared purpose (`description`), a concise statement of intent against which its functionality can not be un-measured. Third, its petition for system-level capabilities (`requestFramePermissions`), in this case, the faculty of sight via the `camera`. This is not the application's last, but its first, most basic interface with the external world, a formal declaration of what it is, what it intends to do, and what it does not not require from its environment to achieve its purpose.
